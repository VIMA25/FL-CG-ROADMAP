FERTYLINK / CRYOGENESIS MONETIZATION STRUCTURES & MODELS

Maximizing EBITDA and lifetime value (LTV) across the HoldCo while reducing customer acquisition costs (CAC) and clinical failure risk.

Having a digital HIPAA-compliant matching engine (Fertylink) paired with a bio-banking tissue entity (CryoGenesis) under one HoldCo umbrella provides a significant strategic advantage. Below is a blueprint for separate monetization models for each LLC, followed by joint strategy models designed to maximize synergistic returns for the HoldCo.

Fertylink LLC: Digital Match, SaaS & Logistics Monetization

As a digital matching platform connecting Intended Parents (IPs), donors, Gestational Carriers (GCs), and clinics, Fertylink operates as an asset-light, scalable ecosystem. Its revenue strategy balances transactional fees, software subscriptions, and high-margin concierge logistics.

CryoGenesis LLC: Gamete Bio-Banking & Outcome Monetization

-Eggs Division-
UNIQUE VALUE PROPOSITION:
EUPLOID EMBRYO(s) GUARANTEED

CryoGenesis is the physical tissue banking arm. Rather than competing on raw, commodity egg counts, CryoGenesis monetizes guaranteed clinical outcomes anchored in precise laboratory funnel math (accounting for vitrification thaw survival, fertilization, PGT-A euploidy rates, and live birth rates per transfer).

1. Egg & Sperm Donation Program outcome guaranteed.

Fresh and frozen eggs: guaranteeing both not tested blasts and euploid blastocyst stage embryos.

2. Double Donor Embryo Program:

Intended Parents select any donor from our existing frozen bio-bank inventory. We execute a synchronized laboratory thaw and fertilization cycle utilizing either proprietary donor sperm or partner-provided specimens. Following successful embryo-culture, the resulting blastocysts stage embryos will be biopsied and vitrified. Upon obtaining the genetic testing results, only the euploid embryos will be dispatched via medical courier to your designated IVF clinic for transfer.

3. EggShare Program

What it is. A woman freezes her eggs through CryoGenesis. We cover her stimulation and retrieval cycle at no cost; in return she shares a portion of her eggs with the bank and keeps the rest.
How it works. She screens; if eligible, CryoGenesis covers and runs her full stimulation and retrieval cycle. Mature eggs are split between participant and bank. Retained eggs are frozen and stored free for the first year. If a cycle doesn't yield enough to reasonably expect at least one euploid, a second cycle is automatically required.
Who it's for. Healthy women, generally 21–34, put off by out-of-pocket freezing cost and open to helping another family (the "missing middle").

Share — Authoritative rules (APPROVED — these govern; all other docs defer)

  1. Free stimulation and retrieval cycle — at no cost.
  2. All cycles free — every cycle under the program is free to the participant.
  3. Automatic second cycle on low yield — if a cycle doesn't yield enough to expect at least one euploid, a second cycle is automatically required.
  4. Egg split, odd number favors CryoGenesis — on an odd total, CryoGenesis receives the majority. (Working target ~50/50; batch model assumes the bank keeps ~10 of ~20 eggs per Giver.)
  5. Free 1 year of cryopreservation — then billed to the participant.
  6. Testing not included — billed to the participant.
  7. Medications not included — billed to the participant.

STRATEGY TO CAPTURE SPERM MARKET SHARE IN THE US & GLOBALLY

To aggressively capture market share from legacy giants like California Cryobank and Fairfax Cryobank, we cannot simply rely on opening a physical tissue bank. We must leverage our digital infrastructure—Fertylink—as our primary customer acquisition and distribution engine.

Here is the strategic blueprint and pricing architecture to successfully launch and scale our retail sperm division on a national level.

1.The Fertylink Distribution Wedge

  • The Freemium SaaS Model: We will aggressively deploy an outsourced sales team to onboard 80 clinics in our first year by offering them Fertylink's clinic management software entirely for free. This provides REIs and clinic administrators with a powerful, free desktop tool to manage their donor and carrier applications, paperwork, filters, and tests.
  • Captive Audience Integration: Once an REI practice adopts Fertylink as their internal donor portal, our CryoGenesis retail sperm inventory is natively embedded into their workflow. We become the path of least resistance for their patients.
  • Mobile-First Access: We will push our retail sperm inventory directly to Intended Parents (IPs) via the Fertylink mobile apps available on Google Play and the Apple App Store.

Currently, national competitors like Fairfax Cryobank are charging between $1,300 and $2,100 per vial, with California Cryobank scaling up to $2,397 for premium IDs. To aggressively penetrate this market, we will undercut their mid-tier pricing while utilizing Fertylink's transaction fee model to protect our HoldCo margins.

Generating a predictable Inventory Model

When we model a donor contract—typically a 6-month commitment—we are optimizing for two primary variables: the clinical preparation type (IUI vs. ART) and the FDA 21 CFR Part 1271 compliance costs, which heavily front-load the expenses.

Here is the operational breakdown of what we can expect from a highly qualified donor collecting once per week over a 6-month (26-week) lifetime, along with the associated acquisition and processing costs.

1.Lifetime Vial Yields (6 Months / 26 Collections)

The "Blended" Production Model: Most successful approach is to split an ejaculated sample, yielding roughly 2 IUI vials and 4 ART vials per week. Over 26 weeks (assuming one collection per week), this blended strategy yields a highly predictable lifetime inventory of ~ (52 IUI vials - $62,140 per donor) and (104 ART vials - $93,080) total 156 total vials ($155,220). Note: assuming one collection per week. If a donor collects 2 samples per week these numbers will double.

2.The 6-Month Cost Floor (Unit Economics)

To generate this inventory, the clinic incurs direct costs divided across three distinct operational phases: Donor Compensation, Clinical Processing, and FDA/Regulatory Screening.

If we apply this total cost floor to our blended production model (~156 vials), the internal Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) per vial, including underperforming donor, sits around $110. And the coast per donor: $17,160.

So, per donor: total revenue: $155,220 - COGS $17,160 = gross profit: $138,060 (assuming one collection per week) and $276,120 (if two collections/week)

When you consider that legacy national banks retail IUI and ART vials for anywhere from $1,000 to over $2,000, the gross margin on a successfully cleared donor routinely exceeds 85%.
